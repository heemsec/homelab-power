YOUR RATE

What do you pay per kWh?

Use the all-in number from your bill. Changing currency only changes the symbol, not the exchange rate. Times are local 24-hour decimals, so 17.5 means 17:30, and the peak window repeats every day. If start and end match, there's no peak. Decimal points and decimal commas both work.

THE BREAKER

Will it trip?

Amps are load watts divided by volts, added up as if everything is working hard at once. That peak is what pops a breaker. The bill further down uses average watts, so this section won't change the cost. For gear that runs for hours, try to stay under 80% of the breaker. Picking a country above fills in 120 V and 15 A for the US, or 230 V and 16 A in Europe.

Measure it once. The guess gets better.

Put a plug meter on the device. Write down the idle draw after it settles, then what it pulls while doing real work. Even better, log kWh for a few days and compare. Count the disks, supply losses, and the switch. The wattage printed on the PSU is a ceiling, not what it actually draws.
